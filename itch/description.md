# When the Machine Sings

*In Taiwan, when the machine sings, you run.*

---

**A short narrative arcade game about Taiwan's garbage trucks — the machines that summon an entire city with Beethoven.**

Every evening across Taiwan, yellow sanitation trucks roll through the alleys playing *Für Elise*. Residents stream out of their apartments, trash bags in hand, to meet them. Miss the music — miss the truck — the trash stays with you another day.

## What you play

- **Five evenings** in a small Taipei walk-up. Mom keeps reminding you. The phone keeps lighting up. The music is still far away — until it isn't.
- **The melody is the mechanic.** *Für Elise* gets louder as the truck approaches. You *listen* for the moment to run.
- **An ending drawn from a true story** — a Taipei sanitation worker who was prosecuted in 2024 for giving a scavenger grandma a rice cooker he found in the recycling. (Court outcome in Dec 2025: three months suspended; Ministry of Justice began drafting amendments in 2026.)

## Controls

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| `E` | Scroll phone (slack) |
| `T` | Watch TV (slack) |
| `Enter` | Head downstairs with the bag |
| `← →` | Move on the street |
| `Space` | Throw bag / advance dialogue |
| `Esc` | Pause |
| `M` | Mute / unmute |

On-screen buttons also work, so you can play with a mouse — or on a phone in landscape mode. Add `?day=3` to the URL to jump to a specific day.

## Made with

- **Phaser 3** + **Vite** — HTML5, runs in any modern browser
- **Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (Nano Banana)** — painted backgrounds and character portraits
- **Suno AI** — original score (Für Elise re-arrangement + tension + ending theme)
- Field-recorded mom voice and truck audio, Taipei
- Built solo in 6 days for Gamedev.js Jam 2026

## Theme

**Machines.** The garbage truck *is* a machine, but in Taiwan it's also a nightly social ritual — a clock, a community signal, a performance of an 18th-century melody by a sanitation vehicle. The game asks: what happens inside the machine's routine? Who keeps it running? What do we miss when we only notice the machine and not the people inside it?
